About

I am a PhD candidate in Atmospheric and Oceanic Science at the University of Maryland, specializing in land surface processes, remote sensing, and Earth system modeling. My research bridges physical understanding with machine learning to advance climate predictability and improve model representations of critical Earth system components.

I develop physics-informed machine learning frameworks to capture spatiotemporal variability in land surface properties and integrate these advances into operational Earth system models. My work combines numerical modeling, satellite observations, reanalysis data, and data-driven methods to enhance subseasonal-to-decadal prediction skill and reduce model uncertainties.

Beyond my PhD research, I am committed to producing reproducible, operationally relevant research products and communicating climate science through peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations, and collaborative international initiatives. I contribute to efforts at the intersection of climate science, geospatial analysis, and machine learning.

Research Areas

Global Land Surface Parameterization

Developing hybrid physics-informed ML frameworks to capture spatiotemporal and subgrid variability in land surface albedo for Earth system models.

Agricultural Decision Support & Crop Phenology

Building ML frameworks for crop phenology prediction and planting date optimization for climate-resilient agricultural decision-making.

Climate-Health Prediction Systems

Quantifying climate-disease relationships through advanced time series analysis and deep learning to enable early warning systems for disease outbreaks.

Air Quality & Atmospheric Composition

Pan-India PM2.5 characterization, source apportionment analysis, and trajectory-based methods to identify dominant emission sources.
